# Engineering decisions

1. **Indexes.** `employees.emp_code` is unique for identity and employee lookups. `{department, emp_code}` supports filtered employee listing; `{department, joined_on}` supports department headcount lookups; `joined_on` supports month-end employee eligibility. The unique `{emp_code, date}` attendance index enforces one record per employee/day and serves monthly queries. `{date, emp_code}` supports attendance sorting and date-ranged analytics; `{emp_code, punch_in}` finds the latest eligible punch-out. I did not add a standalone `status` index: it would not help the required date ordering nearly as much as the compound date index.
2. **Punch-in race.** Both requests validate the employee and derive the same IST attendance date. They both attempt the insert; MongoDB's unique `{emp_code, date}` index serializes the conflict. One insert succeeds with 201, and the other gets `DuplicateKeyError`, translated to 409. There is no check-then-insert dependency.
3. **Ties.** `$rank` assigns tied employees the same competition rank. The limit filters by rank, not row count, so every employee tied at the cutoff is returned; `emp_code` provides stable ordering.
4. **Headcount.** Department summaries start with eligible employees and look up attendance records. Preserving employees with no matching log means they still contribute once to headcount and zero to the attendance measures.
5. **100x scale.** I would move high-volume reporting to pre-aggregated daily summaries and measure indexes against production-like explain plans, rather than repeatedly scanning raw attendance history.
